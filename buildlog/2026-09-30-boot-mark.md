# A light boot screen: the mark, only what changed

The orbit loading screen runs at 8–10 fps on the Pico (2026-09-26: 100–130 ms a frame, about
50 ms of it the 153,600-byte frame over SPI at 24 MHz on the 2.8"). `firmware/bootmark.py` is the
default boot art now: the orbit's end mark (five owners, three sign, the triangle fills green, two
stay amber, PicoQuorum under it) as flat shapes, with the real status on the bottom line.

- **Dirty rectangles.** Each element (a dot, a ring, an edge, the fill, the name, the status) has a
  small "look" tuple for time t. A frame compares looks with the last frame's and repaints only the
  rectangles of those that changed: the whole scene drawn into lcd's own buffer as a window of that
  size (as many rows as fit), then sent with CASET/RASET to just that window. No new buffers.
- **Exact.** An edge repaints from its start to its new end, not only the new piece: a shorter
  Bresenham line is not a prefix of a longer one, and the piece-only version left stray pixels
  (caught by the test at 1350 ms). The test compares the panel with a fresh paint after every
  frame, on the 240x240, 320x240 and 160x128 panels.
- **Numbers (emulator, 30 ms steps, 131 frames).** 240x240: 6.9% of whole-frame bytes; 320x240:
  5.5%; 160x128: 8.2%. Busiest frame after the first: 26 KB / 30 KB / 14 KB. 17 frames send
  nothing. In the emulator, where SPI time is modelled and the CPU is much faster, a mark frame
  averages 2.7 ms against the orbit's 54 ms. On the Pico, expect a few ms to ~15 ms a frame;
  **not yet measured on the board.**
- **Timing.** 0.3 s black, 3.8 s of animation, then the console (the orbit held 4 s + 9.6 s).
  `splash.spin` redraws at most every 30 ms for the mark (120 ms for the orbit), and `net.connect`
  now calls its progress every 50 ms (the LED still toggles every 150 ms), so the mark keeps moving
  while WiFi joins.
- **Choices.** `secrets.BOOT_ART = "mark" | "still" | "orbit" | "checklist"`. "still" draws the
  finished mark once; after that only a status change is sent (13 KB on the 240x240). "orbit" is
  only for strip-buffered panels of 240 rows; elsewhere it falls back to the mark.
- `bootmark` is released at `splash.finish` like `bootart`, and is in all three images in
  `firmware/manifest.json`. Suites: manifest, bootart (pinned to `BOOT_ART="orbit"`), bootmark,
  display, console, ceremony, trustm, micropython all pass.
- **Taglines** (`firmware/taglines.py`): 16 serious (each a claim the firmware backs: the hash
  rebuilt on the device, a lying service refused, the key made in the chip and never a seed phrase,
  plain words, the verify code) and 20 fun (Hackers, Silicon Valley, The Matrix, WarGames, Sneakers,
  Jurassic Park, Mr. Robot, Zero Wing, cyberpunk). One per boot under the name on the mark, cycling on
  the orbit. `secrets.BOOT_TAGLINES = "serious" | "fun" | "all" | "none"`, serious by default. All
  fit 220 px in sans14 ("Mess with the best, die like the rest" was 223 and was cut). No tagline on
  the 1.8". Released with the art at `splash.finish`.
