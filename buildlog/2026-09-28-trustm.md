# The OPTIGA Trust M beside the ATECC608

The Trust M batch (Adafruit 4351) arrived. One board is on the Build 1 bench, on I2C0 (GP4/GP5)
with no ATECC. Goals: the board finds out which chip it has at boot and signs with either, and
the Trust M gets a ceremony. Decisions and the profile are in docs/TRUSTM.md.

## Read-only probe first

A script run from RAM over mpremote, with upstream's `trustm.py` inlined; nothing written:
- `i2c.scan()` returned `[]`, with and without the ATECC wake token. **A sleeping Trust M NACKs
  its address.** `signer.detect()` therefore polls 0x30's state register as well as scanning.
- UID `cd16338401001c000100000a091b5c000b0062006c801010710809`: firmware 80101071, build 0809,
  wafer 98,108. LcsA operational, security event counter 0.
- Certificate (E0E0) from "Infineon OPTIGA(TM) Trust M CA 101", serial `1d560a9b`.
- **E0F1:** empty, LcsO creation, Change `LcsO<op`, Execute ALW.
- **F1D0–F1DB:** 140 bytes each (100 used, zeros), with the same Change.
- **E0F0:** Change NEV, P-256, usage Auth.

The factory Change on E0F1, F1D0 and F1D1 is already `LcsO < operational`. So the seal is only
raising their LcsO, with no access-condition writes at all.

## Built (emulator first)

- **Drivers and pure logic:**
  - `trustm_q.py`: GenKeyPair, SetDataObject, lifecycle, metadata and the last error code, on upstream's unchanged `trustm.py`.
  - `tmcfg.py`: metadata, certificate, record and binding, and the screen lines. Pure logic.
- **Signer:**
  - `signer.detect()` and `load(pins=)`.
  - `TrustMSigner`, and the console interface on both signers.
- **Console:** `quorum.py` goes through that interface.
- **The Trust M ceremony:** `provision_tm.py`, T1–T8, on the ATECC ceremony's screen (`provision.UI`, now shared).
- **Routing:** `main_dual` and `chipapp` send a fresh Trust M to it.
- **Emulator and tests:**
  - `trustm_sim.py`: a virtual Trust M at 0x30, with the real chip's factory state.
  - `test-vectors/run-trustm.mjs`: 70 checks.
- **Host tooling:** `tools/fw` reads either chip, and `pin` writes `slot: 0xE0F1` for a Trust M.
- **Signing screens:** they show the chip's own facts (Infineon Trust M, CC EAL6+, E0F1 sealed, bound to the Infineon cert, the security event counter).

## On the real chip

Firmware mounted from the laptop (`mpremote mount`), so the board's flash was never written.

**Detection, certificate and E0F0:**
- Detection found the Trust M, and `load()` gave a TrustMSigner.
- **The certificate verified against the pinned CA 101 key on the Pico in 1.4 s.**
- E0F0 signed, and the signature verified under the certificate's key. CalcSign takes about 150 ms, including the session.

**The security event counter:**
- **+1 per CalcSign**, for E0F0 and for E0F1, on these bench runs. The console run later showed this is only true once the chip's idle credit is spent (see below).
- Nothing for refusals (bad OID, CalcSign on an empty key, reading a key object).
- **It fades about 1 every 5 s.**
- The first sim assumed "unchanged", and so did the console's check. Both now expect +1 per signature and flag only more than that.

**Error codes:** 0x01 bad OID, 0x07 reading a key object, 0x0B CalcSign on an empty key. The sim now matches.

**The ceremony, T1–T6, headless from the laptop** (ALLOW_GENKEY injected in RAM only): all passed.
- New key `ef677807…` in E0F1.
- The binding verifies under the certificate.
- The record and the binding read back.
- **Owner `0x47c0998C6a7A1955794071b9f4058e7814178f35`**, the same on the Pico and from viem on the laptop.
- **Nothing is sealed:** E0F1, F1D0 and F1D1 are still at LcsO creation.

## Later the same day: the ceremony and the console on the board

- **The ceremony screens were black on the 2.8" panel.** They drew once instead of strip by strip.
  - Fixed, and the knob now drives the ceremony (`41b14201`).
- **T1 on the device stopped.** The headless run's snapshot was on the laptop.
  - T1 now resumes a ceremony started elsewhere (`d34020ea`).
  - The snapshot survives pushes (`07f0d45e`).
- **T1–T6 went green on the device.**
  - The owner shown at T5 matches `getSigner` on Base Sepolia and Base: `0x47c0998C6a7A1955794071b9f4058e7814178f35`.
  - No signer contract is deployed yet.
- **The console on the real Trust M,** with no chain involved:
  - Setup: TM1 pinned with `locked: False`; `secrets.py` pointed at the laptop's mock Safe (the real lines are kept on the board as `secrets.pretm`).
  - At boot: the binding verified in 2.6 s, and 147 KB of heap was free.
  - #0 approved on the device. The mock verified the WebAuthn P-256 signature, and #0 executed. **The first Safe approval by a Trust M.**
- **The security event counter read "0 -> 0" for that signature.**
  - Probing the running console: after about 10 minutes idle, 8 signatures left it at 0, then it rose by 1 per signature (7 after 12).
  - So **key uses are paid from an idle credit first.** The first bench runs had no credit.
  - The Sending screen now reports the counter's actual change instead of "+1, this signature". The sim models the credit.
