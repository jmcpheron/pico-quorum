New build · PicoQuorum Handheld

A signing console you hold in two hands.

A Pico 2 W, a 2.4″ screen, a knob and a Sign button under a flip cover, in a flat printed case you hold like a handheld game or lay on the desk. The key, in a snap-shut clamshell, plugs into the top edge.

What's in it

Size
86 × 118 × 30 mm; 164 mm tall with a key in
Board
Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W, wires soldered to its pads
Screen
2.4″ IPS, 240 × 320, SPI
Controls
An EC11 knob to turn and push. A tactile Sign button under a flip cover
Key
key-c, an ATECC608C behind USB-C, in a clamshell
Case
Eight printed parts and the key's two, M3 screws into heat-set inserts
Firmware
The console image as is: BOARD = "build1", SCREEN = "2.4"

A first draft. Designed and checked in CAD: 223 pairs of parts clear each other. Nothing is printed, wired or run yet, and the 2.4″ screen hasn't been seen working. Measure your parts first; the README says which.

Same console, same rules

  1. 1

    Turn to read

    The knob pages through what a transaction does, in plain language. Owner and threshold changes are red pages.

  2. 2

    Lift the cover

    Sign sits in a well under a flip cover, so it isn't pressed by accident.

  3. 3

    Hold to sign

    Only after every page is seen, and only while held: 2 s, or 3 s when a page is red. Let go early and nothing is signed.

  4. 4

    The key stays in its chip

    key-c signs the Safe transaction hash the console worked out itself. Its private key never leaves the chip.

Four colours from one plate

The front plate prints face down, so its face is its first layers. Change filament three times, at 0.6, 1.2 and 1.8 mm, and each depth of cut shows its own colour: the name in charcoal, TURN and SIGN in red, the wells in blue. No paint, no inlays.

The depths, colours and words are settings in handheld.py. Not checked on a real print yet.

Build it

Sixteen steps, from printed parts to a key in the top. Each picture is drawn from the case's own model, so it follows the CAD's numbers: new printed parts in amber, what's done in white, and what the step needs along the bottom.

The full guide, with every detail The STLs

What you need

  • Bought: a Pico 2 W, the 2.4″ screen, an EC11 knob and cap, a 12 × 12 mm tactile switch and a mini breadboard, a USB-C plug on a short cable, two 4.7 kΩ resistors, a key-c.
  • Hardware: eight M3 heat-set inserts, eight M3 × 8 and six M2 × 8 socket heads, four M3 washers, four rubber feet, 20 mm of 1.75 mm filament for the hinge.
  • Tools: a soldering iron with an insert tip, 2.5 and 1.5 mm hex keys, flush cutters, a meter.

The tub

  1. 1. An insert in each corner post

    Let each one sink in under the iron's own weight. Don't twist. Stop when it's flush.

  2. 2. The feet

    Four rubber strips into their pockets, round ends into the round ends.

  3. 3. The key's USB-C plug

    Pull-ups soldered at the plug first. Then up through the top edge until it's flush, and the lock clicks in under it.

  4. 4. The breadboard and the Sign switch

    Between its four stops. The switch goes on the 3rd column from the right: that's under the button.

The plate

  1. 5. An insert in each of the carrier's bosses

    Plate face down on a towel. Same as step 1.

  2. 6. The Sign button's guard

    The cover hinges on a pin of 1.75 mm filament. The cap goes up through the base from underneath.

  3. 7. The guard into its well

    Two M2 screws. Shut, the cover is flush with the face.

  4. 8. The knob

    In from behind, its nut on in the well. Snug, not tight.

  5. 9. The screen onto its pins

    Glass down, its 8-pin header on the left.

  6. 10. The spacers

    A printed spacer on each pin.

  7. 11. The carrier

    Four M3 screws with washers. Tighten them evenly: they hold the screen down.

  8. 12. The Pico

    Solder its wires first, with the board in your hand. Then onto the posts, USB to the right edge.

  9. 13. The wires

    Screen and knob to the Pico. Try it on the bench before it goes in the tub.

Close it, and the key

  1. 14. Onto the tub

    The key's plug and the button's jumpers to the Pico. Then the plate on, four socket heads into the counterbores.

  2. 15. The key into its clamshell

    A peg through the key's lanyard hole, the cover on until four clips click. The chip shows through a window.

  3. 16. The knob's cap, then the key

    Straight down onto the plug in the top edge, its chip's window toward the face. Done.

Then: make it an owner

Built, it's a PicoQuorum console like any other. Run the bench check, set it up for your Safe, and add its key as one owner. The owner's guide walks through it, and the app checks every confirmation and executes.
