# Case ideas

Three ways to box the stompbox. A is modelled in [`case.py`](case.py); B and C are only written down.

| | A: the pedal | B: the desk wedge | C: the open sled |
|---|---|---|---|
| looks like | a guitar effects pedal | a small desk console | a bare board on a plate |
| footswitch | front, stomped or pressed by hand | a bar along the front edge | front corner |
| screen | landscape, behind the controls | landscape, tilted about 25° | flat, on standoffs |
| key | stands up out of the lid at the back | plugs in from the side, like a cartridge | on a short cable, loose |
| print | lid ~1.5 h, base ~4 h (0.28 mm layers) | ~5 h | ~1 h |
| for tonight | yes: print the lid first | too long | the fallback |

## A: the pedal (modelled)

A Hammond-1590-style wedge, about 100 × 150 mm. The footswitch at the front says what the box
is for before anyone reads the screen: signing is a deliberate act, something you stand on. The
key standing up at the back shows in every shot, so a key swap reads on camera.

Next steps if it's the one:
- a round fillet along the lid's top edges
- a clip under the plug's sleeve instead of hot glue
- a printed bracket for the screen on its own holes, once it's measured
- a chamfer on the back wall's USB hole

## B: the desk wedge

The screen tilted up to face you, the knob to its right, and a long hinged bar across the front edge
over the footswitch, like a piano's sustain pedal turned upside down. The key slides into a slot in
the left side and its end shows, like a game cartridge. Better for close-up filming of the screen;
less of a story than a stomp.

## C: the open sled

A 3 mm plate with standoffs: the Pico, the screen and the knob in plain view, the footswitch in a
printed block at the front, the key on a short USB-C cable. Every wire shows, which suits a "this is
homemade, here's everything in it" post. One hour to print, so it's the fallback if A's lid doesn't
fit tonight.
